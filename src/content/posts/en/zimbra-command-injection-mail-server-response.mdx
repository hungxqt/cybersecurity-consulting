On 30 September 2026, Microsoft reported exploitation of CVE-2026-73570 in Zimbra's SNMP notification path. The described exposure requires the optional zimbra-snmp package and enabled SNMP notifications; exploitation does not require user interaction. [Read Microsoft's investigation and mitigation guidance](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/09/30/unauthenticated-command-injection-on-internet-facing-mail-servers-tracking-cve-2026-73570/).

## Our assessment: separate exposure from compromise

An exposed configuration establishes a remediation need. It does not, by itself, prove the server was breached. Conversely, updating software cannot establish that an earlier intrusion has been removed.

Maintain two workstreams with separate owners. The platform owner verifies affected components and schedules a supported correction. The incident responder determines whether available evidence shows unauthorized execution, persistence or access to mail.

## Record enough evidence to make a decision

Our recommended investigation record covers the installed package inventory, notification settings, upgrade history and relevant log retention. Preserve evidence before changes where operationally feasible; do not delay urgent containment solely to produce a perfect record.

Review unexpected child processes of the mail service, newly created services, changes in application directories and unusual outbound transfers. Correlate findings with maintenance tickets. A known administrative command can become meaningful when it appears under an unexpected service account or outside its approved window.

## Close both workstreams

Follow the current vendor advisory for your supported release. Have the platform owner confirm the corrected version and configuration rather than assuming deployment success.

If investigation identifies unauthorized access, scope affected accounts and data, remove persistence and revoke relevant access under the incident plan. Keep a written distinction between confirmed findings, unresolved questions and evidence that was unavailable.

Close the exposure ticket with configuration evidence. Close the incident only after the responder can explain the remaining risk and the business owner accepts it.
