ENISA published its 2026 Threat Landscape on 22 September, analyzing incidents observed during January–December 2025. The agency emphasizes interconnected digital dependencies and their effect on resilience. A 2026 publication date should not be confused with a 2026 incident dataset. [Read ENISA's report scope](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/publications/enisa-threat-landscape-2026).

## Our assessment: assess the service behind the supplier

A supplier review often stops at a questionnaire or certificate. Those documents can support assurance, but they do not show which business services would fail if the supplier became unavailable.

Choose a critical service and map its dependencies: identity provider, DNS, hosting, payment processing, remote support and data exchange. Include shared components used by several suppliers. Two contracted providers may still depend on the same underlying service.

## Give each dependency a recovery decision

Our recommended record connects each dependency to:

- The business process and data it supports.
- The access it has to your environment.
- A named operational and commercial owner.
- The impact of losing it and the expected recovery path.
- Evidence of a tested alternative, or an accepted limitation.

Review this record with engineering and procurement together. Contract language, technical design and response procedures need to describe a compatible recovery plan.

## Test a disruption that crosses ownership boundaries

Use an authorized tabletop exercise in which a supplier loses service or reports a breach. Ask who verifies the report, who can restrict access and how the business continues if recovery takes longer than expected.

Document decisions that require the supplier's participation and confirm the contact channel in advance. The result should be a realistic recovery plan and a short list of unresolved dependencies with owners. This is how a broad threat landscape becomes a specific assurance conversation for your business.
