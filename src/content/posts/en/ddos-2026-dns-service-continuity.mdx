Cloudflare's report, published on 11 August 2026 and covering January–June, says DNS-based vectors accounted for 34.3% of network-layer DDoS attacks it observed. The percentage describes Cloudflare's dataset, rather than every attack worldwide. [Read Cloudflare's H1 2026 report](https://blog.cloudflare.com/ddos-threat-report-2026-h1/).

## Our assessment: test the route the customer depends on

A website can have healthy servers while customers cannot reach it. DNS, upstream connectivity, identity and application dependencies can each interrupt a transaction before it reaches the application.

Map the route from name resolution to a completed customer action. Identify where traffic is protected, where it can bypass protection and which provider owns each part of the path. Include externally hosted sign-in and payment services when they are needed to complete the transaction.

## Prepare decisions before traffic spikes

Our recommended readiness review asks:

- Which team can change protective controls and contact the provider?
- Can customers still resolve the service name during a DNS disruption?
- Is the origin reachable through a route that bypasses the intended protection?
- Which rate limits preserve legitimate customer behavior?
- Where will status updates appear if the main site is unavailable?

Document the answers with contact details and change authority. Evaluate provider capacity and contractual coverage against your architecture rather than assuming a product name covers every dependency.

## Verify continuity with controlled checks

Use authorized load and failover checks agreed with the infrastructure provider. Measure customer outcomes such as sign-in and checkout completion alongside latency and error rates.

Review false blocks and the recovery time after protective settings are removed. Give the business owner the result as a service-continuity decision: what remains available, what degrades and what requires manual action. Availability becomes an operational capability when those decisions have owners and evidence.
