Most teams measure a security operations centre by how many alerts it closes. That number goes up when detections get noisier, which means the metric rewards exactly the wrong behaviour.

## What a closed alert does not tell you

An alert closed in 90 seconds could be a correct dismissal or a missed intrusion. The queue cannot tell them apart, and neither can a dashboard that counts throughput.

Three numbers tell a better story:

- **Time to detect (MTTD).** From first malicious action to the first alert a human sees.
- **Time to contain (MTTR).** From that alert to the attacker losing access.
- **Escalation precision.** The share of escalated alerts that turned out to be real.

## Tune by removing noise, not by raising thresholds

Raising a threshold hides the weak signal along with the noise. Instead, work down your noisiest rule: add the context that separates benign from hostile (asset owner, expected scheduler, known scanner) and keep the rule sensitive.

## A weekly habit that pays off

Pick ten closed alerts at random each week and have a second analyst re-triage them cold. The disagreement rate is the most honest quality signal a SOC has.
